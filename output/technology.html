Famed 'Heat' Director Michael Mann Hospitalized After Gun Range Accident

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Michael Mann has been hospitalized following an accident at a Los Angeles gun range.

“On Saturday, at a gun range, a weapon accidentally discharged while holstered resulting in a superficial injury to film director Michael Mann,” a rep for the director, 83, confirmed in a written statement shared with Us on Sunday, November 1. “He was treated and is being released.” (TMZ was first to report the news.)

Sources familiar with the incident told TMZ the director was “trying to holster his weapon during target practice” at “around 4pm” on Saturday, October 31, while patronizing the members-only shooting club The Range LA, resulting in The Last of the Mohicans director accidentally shooting himself. He was transported to a nearby hospital via ambulance where he was treated for subcutaneous and minor injuries.

Mann, an experienced target shooter who has been practicing for decades, is best known for his work on such beloved movies as Heat, Collateral, Manhunter, Miami Vice, Public Enemies, 20203’s Ferrari — featuring Adam Driver — and more.

He is currently working on a sequel to his hit 1995 crime classic Heat, which is scheduled to begin filming early next year, per CBS News. The director recently opened up about aging and the advancement of technology — specifically artificial intelligence — in Hollywood as he continues to maintain steady work well into his 80s.

Michael Mann Getty Images

“I don’t experiment with technology gratuitously,” Mann said during an October press conference at the Lumière Festival in Lyon, where he received the event’s honorary award for his decades of directorial work. “If I have a dramatic or aesthetic need for it, then I go deep into what I need. For example, aging and de-aging will be very important in Heat 2.”

He continued at the time, “Heat 2 is an expensive movie to make, but I believe it should be made at the proper size and scale. It’s complex… shooting in Chicago, Los Angeles, Paraguay, and possibly Singapore.”

The sequel will reportedly reintroduce fans to the lives of Detective Vincent Hanna (Al Pacino), Neil McCauley (Robert De Niro) and Chris Shiherlis (the late Val Kilmer) during a time that pre-dates the original. Allowing the original actors to convincingly portray their younger selves in the film means, according to Mann, the use of modern AI tools. (Kilmer died in April 2025 at the age of 65.)

“The characters of Heat are so alive to me … I know everything about these characters,” he said. “I always wanted to do more with these people but I couldn’t figure out how to do it because one’s dead at the end of Heat.”

Obsession's Inde Navarrette Shades Shane Gillis' Gym Comments on 'SNL'

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Obsession star Inde Navarette made her Saturday Night Live debut with a not-so-subtle dig at the last host, Shane Gillis, and his controversial monologue remarks about women’s fitness apparel.

“I loved making Obsession, and I can’t believe I’m saying this, but I’m gonna be in a Marvel movie,” Navarette, 25, began her Saturday, October 31, monologue, referring to her recent casting as Rogue in the new X-Men reboot. “I know it’s really important for Marvel that you get really fit, so I’ve been working out a lot.”

She continued, “So, every morning I put on my little whore outfit and I hit the gym.”

Josh Hutcherson Remembers 'SNL' Being His '1st-Ever Live Audience'

Navarette’s SNL gig directly follows Gillis, 38, who hosted an episode of the long-running hit variety show earlier in October. In his monologue, he lamented about women’s workout gear.

“I haven’t been to the gym in so long, I didn’t know women just dress like whores now? Look, I’m sorry, there’s no other way to describe [it],” the comedian complained at the time. “Have you seen it? I’ve seen it. You’ve seen the shorts? They’re those neon, like, artificial bait-colored [shorts], and the wrinkles go around the cheeks and all combine at the butthole. And don’t look! You’re not allowed to look.”

Gillis’ comments drew instant backlash.

“Women wearing short shorts to the gym does not make them whores. It makes the men calling them whores misogynists. There’s a difference there,” Schitt’s Creek alum Dan Levy responded to Gillis in a social media video. “I’m speechless that something like that can exist on live national television. To be perfectly honest, more men need to be standing up in opposition to it because it’s complacency that has created the environment [in] which women are sexually assaulted, and it’s a conversation as opposed to a crime.”

Levy, 43, further thought more men should defend women instead of tearing apart their appearances.
